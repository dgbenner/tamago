# Tamago: A1 Complete

Draft 1, November 10, 2026. Goal: finishing a course's A1 means the learner is at A1, measured against official
lists and real A1 tasks. This replaces Duolingo-style points with a level people understand.

Applies to French, Spanish and German. Framework is built once; content is built per language.

## 1. A1 in parts

- The current tree becomes **A1 · Part 1**.
- Build further parts until the course covers its official A1 list (section 3). Expect about 3 to 4 parts per
  language; the builder sets the number from the list and the gap.
- Each part is about 10 to 15 circles, with checkpoints and help circles as now.
- **The tree shows one part at a time.** It ends with a cap: "End of A1 · Part 1 of 4". Below the cap, the next
  part shows as one locked banner with its title and a one-line summary of what it covers, not its circles.
- **The next part unlocks** when every circle in the current part has finished round 1 (the same rule as rows,
  R20). Finished parts stay open and can be revisited from a part switcher at the top of the tree.
- The final A1 checkpoint (the DELF A1, DELE A1 or Start Deutsch 1 format) moves to the end of the last part.
  Each part ends with a short part review instead.

## 2. Blend, don't box

- Circles are themed around real situations (Finding an Address, At the Market, Making Plans), not rigid
  categories.
- Each themed circle mixes what the situation needs: its words, its grammar point, a task (section 4), and the
  communication strategies that fit ("Can you spell that?", "How do you say …?").
- Keep the three help circles per part as anchors, but strategies also appear inside themed circles wherever
  they belong.
- Titles stay 2 to 3 words, Title Case, and say the situation.

## 3. Words: measured against official lists

- Target list per language:
  - German: Goethe-Institut Start Deutsch 1 word list.
  - Spanish: Instituto Cervantes curriculum plan (PCIC), A1 inventory.
  - French: the DELF A1 reference level for French.
- Store each list in the workbook with its source. Measure coverage: the share of list words the course
  teaches, each in at least 3 sentences.
- Target: 90% or more of the list by the end of the last part.
- Numbers to 100, prices, times, days, months and dates are part of A1 and get their own coverage.

## 4. A1 tasks

Each task is taught in at least one themed circle and tested in the final checkpoint.

| ID | Task |
| --- | --- |
| T01 | Introduce yourself and someone else |
| T02 | Spell your name; give and understand phone numbers |
| T03 | Give and understand age, address and nationality |
| T04 | Numbers, prices and quantities when shopping |
| T05 | Times, days and dates; making a simple plan |
| T06 | Ask and answer about home, family and work |
| T07 | Order food and drink |
| T08 | Ask for and follow simple directions |
| T09 | Understand signs, notices, menus and timetables |
| T10 | Fill in a simple form |
| T11 | Write a short message or postcard (about 30 to 40 words) |
| T12 | Understand short announcements and simple instructions |
| T13 | Ask for help: repeat, slower, spell, how do you say |

## 5. New question types (framework)

| ID | Type | Skill | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| QT14 | Read a short text: a menu, sign, timetable or message, then 2 to 3 questions | Reading | Texts built from known words; Tatoeba lines where they fit, otherwise drafted and marked |
| QT15 | Fill in a form: name, age, address, nationality, phone | Writing | Checked by rules (format, spelling of known words) |
| QT16 | Write a short message to a prompt | Writing | Claude gives feedback on meaning, grammar and missing points; not scored at first; counts as done |
| QT17 | Listen and write the number, price, time or date | Listening | Answers checked as numbers, not text |

All four follow the existing rules: no timers, hints never cost a strike, swaps for Can't Listen Now, and the
same answer bar.

## 6. Progress page

- Show **A1 coverage** per language: words (for example "A1 words: 38%"), tasks ("A1 tasks: 4 of 13"), and the
  part ("Part 1 of 4").
- Keep the per-skill levels. The final checkpoint result shows per skill.

## 7. Quality

- Content follows the existing rules: Tatoeba first, Wiktionary, Lingua Libre, drafts only as a last resort.
- Each new part runs the quality pass in each language: candidates file, Fable's judgment, then the swap.
- The craft review runs once on the new framework (parts, cap, banner and the four new question types).

## 8. Order of work

1. Load the official lists and report current coverage per language. Stop and show Dan the numbers.
2. Framework: parts, the cap and banner, the part switcher, QT14 to QT17, A1 coverage on Progress.
3. Part 2 for one language first (French), with its quality pass. Stop for Dan to try it.
4. Then Part 2 for Spanish and German, then further parts.
