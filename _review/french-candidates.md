November 10, 2026. Each sentence below fits its unit on every other rule the pull applies (every word known by then or a listed name, 2 to 7 words, no digits, articles before a noun or adjective, linked English that carries the unit's nouns, not already in the course, not turned down in review). The only thing missing is the trust signal: its owner hasn't declared French native, and it has no OK tag. Nothing has been swapped.

**In all:** 277 candidates across the units. Of the 293 drafts, 16 are word for word on Tatoeba from an unchecked owner, and 97 have a candidate that teaches all the same new words.

Under each unit: its drafts, each beside the candidates that could replace it: the same sentence on Tatoeba first, then up to three that teach all the draft's new words; then the unit's other fitting candidates. These haven't had my review for idioms or odd lines; they pass the rules only. Links go to Tatoeba, where the owner and any comments can be checked.

**Review, November 10, 2026.** Every candidate shown below was checked for grammar, how it sounds to a French speaker, A1 fit and a true match with its English, and marked **Use** or **Reject** with a reason. Marks are keyed to the Tatoeba sentence, so a sentence that sits beside several drafts carries the same mark each time. Of the 291 distinct sentences shown, 233 are Use and 58 are Reject; 47 of the Use marks name the English to show instead of Tatoeba's line (Marie for Mary, like for love, have for have got).
