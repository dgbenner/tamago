# Spanish: Tatoeba sentences kept out only by the trust rule

November 10, 2026. Each sentence below fits its unit on every other rule the pull applies (every word known by then or a listed name, 2 to 7 words, no digits, articles before a noun or adjective, linked English that carries the unit's nouns, not already in the course, not turned down in review). The only thing missing is the trust signal: its owner hasn't declared Spanish native, and it has no OK tag. Nothing has been swapped.

**In all:** 472 candidates across the units. Of the 158 drafts, 13 are word for word on Tatoeba from an unchecked owner, and 69 have a candidate that teaches all the same new words.

Under each unit: its drafts, each beside the candidates that could replace it: the same sentence on Tatoeba first, then up to three that teach all the draft's new words; then the unit's other fitting candidates. These haven't had my review for idioms or odd lines; they pass the rules only. Links go to Tatoeba, where the owner and any comments can be checked.

**Review, November 10, 2026.** Every candidate shown below was checked for grammar, Latin American naturalness, A1 fit and a true match with its English, and marked **Use** or **Reject** with a reason. Marks are keyed to the Tatoeba sentence, so a sentence that sits beside several drafts carries the same mark each time. Of the 398 distinct sentences shown, 283 are Use and 115 are Reject. Of the 69 drafts that had a candidate, 62 now have at least one usable replacement; S3102, S3109, S3110, S5113, S9101, S9102, S14104 lost every candidate they had. The "and N more" tails are not in this file and were not reviewed. A "Use" with an English pick means the Spanish is sound but the course should show the named English instead of the Tatoeba line (María for Mary, like for love). Nothing has been swapped.
