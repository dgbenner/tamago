# Polish — A1 scouting report

## 1. Grammar features that differ from courses already built
- **7 cases** (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental, locative, vocative). These inflect *everyday* A1 nouns: "I have a dog" needs the accusative (`psa`), "I don't have a dog" the genitive (`psa` → genitive after negation). Case is unavoidable from lesson 1.
- **No articles at all** — no definite/indefinite distinction to teach.
- **3 genders** (masculine / feminine / neuter), with masculine further split by animacy (animate vs inanimate) and personhood. Adjectives agree in gender, number *and* case.
- **Verbal aspect** — nearly every verb comes as a perfective/imperfective pair. This is a core A1 concept with no analogue in the Romance/Germanic courses.
- **Numerals govern case**: 2–4 take nominative plural, 5+ take genitive plural. The numbers circle needs a dedicated note.
- **Script**: Latin + `ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż` and digraphs `sz cz rz dz`. Nasal vowels `ą ę`.

## 2. What the A1 circles / grammar notes need
- **Glossary**: the single "article/gender" slot breaks. Replace with gender **+ declension class**, and ideally show nominative + genitive + accusative (the A1-critical trio).
- **Colours/adjectives circle**: must teach adjective↔noun agreement across case + gender, not just lookup forms.
- **Verb circles**: need a new aspect note; present-tense conjugation by person; gendered past tense.
- **Numbers circle**: numeral-governed case note (above).
- No article means any "a/the" scaffolding from existing courses is simply dropped.
- **Wiktionary friction**: English Wiktionary has rich Polish declension tables + gender/animacy, so facts exist — but the pipeline currently reads one article/gender and must be extended to parse full case tables.

## 3. Word list
- **No clean, freely-downloadable CEFR-graded A1 list.** The **KELLY project** covered Polish and assigns CEFR levels in principle, but there is no stable public download and the license is murky — some KELLY lists are cited as **CC BY-NC-SA 2.0** (non-commercial, a problem if Tamago is commercial). Source: https://ssharoff.github.io/kelly/ , https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Frequency_lists/Polish
- **Reliable stand-in**: **hermitdave FrequencyWords** (OpenSubtitles2018), content **CC BY-SA 4.0** (code MIT). Covers Polish. Source: https://github.com/hermitdave/FrequencyWords (frequency-ranked, not CEFR-graded).

## 4. Tatoeba + Lingua Libre coverage
- **Tatoeba: ~137,254 Polish sentences** — healthy, so most circles can be filled from checked sentences. Source: https://tatoeba.org/en/stats/sentences_by_language
- **Lingua Libre: ~98,587 Polish recordings** (via Wikimedia Commons `Category:Lingua Libre pronunciation-pol`) — strong human-audio coverage, little TTS fallback needed.

**Verdict: HIGH.** Biggest obstacle: the 7-case system plus verbal aspect, which breaks the one-word-article/simple-gender Glossary model and adds concepts A1 learners meet immediately. Corpus health is the bright spot.
